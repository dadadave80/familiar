# Product

## Platform
web

## Stack
User specified Vite, React, TypeScript, Phaser 3 for the town canvas, vanilla CSS, self-hosted open fonts, NEAR client signing, and a PWA manifest. Bun manages packages, scripts, tests, and services. Vercel is the requested web hosting target.

## Users
Curious people who will never open a crypto wallet. Secondary audience: hackathon judges trying the game in two minutes, including AI reproducibility checks.

## Product Purpose
First Light is Familiar's current adventure: guide Mochi through tide decisions, bring discoveries home, deliberately restore the harbor, and choose a meaningful second goal. Storycraft makes an earned lantern personal through Crest/Moth, two home sockets and a static origin. The whole-screen game world uses custom coastal navigation and controls. The local preview works before sign-in; canonical progress and transferable ownership require the separately verified testnet stack and recoverable account. No recovery claim follows from local saves or app login alone.

Bell Steps and Reed Steps connect the restored harbor to supply and skiff crossings. After the signal and both services are safely restored, Bram clears the Bell arch. Following Mochi's attention there at low tide reveals a bell. A safe return rigs a single-use pull for future Bell/Reed outings, changing the next surge and bridge route. These side outings and shared discoveries remain device-local, separate from accepted H1 progress. Mochi's contextual idle attention and optional hand greeting spend no decisions and earn no currency or affection points.

The introduction remains the homepage. Play Familiar opens `/outing/`, which starts on a title screen inside the game; Continue or New Harbor hands off to the spatial game and never erases a save. Once the beacon is lit, the harbor card leads to today’s tide run: Today’s tide shows the day’s card, Bram’s guess and the three stamps, and a finished tide opens the Home sheet with its result. Saved harbors keep the Tidekeeper’s three-ask court as their daily; on device harbors it stays reachable from the Chart. Retired town, motion-preview, recovery-demo and classic-game entry points no longer offer separate games. Historical rules and signing/ownership code remain where required to verify old earned returns and original operations; their presence does not expose an old playable edition.

Bram’s shelf joins the same daily loop: bring reed or glass home from today’s tide run (up to three of each; pearls stay with Mochi), shelve it, then trade for Shells to fill Mochi’s wish. On saved harbors Bram shelves Beacon Court hauls every other day; a find kept on this device can wait until tomorrow. Signed out, shelving and trades are a labelled device preview; wishes need a saved harbor.

## Operating Context
First Light fills the desktop viewport and fits mobile portrait/landscape. Keep the whole page from scrolling; useful contained menus may scroll. The current court connects three columns of platforms across two banks. Each decision advances the tide; the marked source predicts the next wave, which moves the bridge and exposes discoveries. Retrieved cargo must reach the dock. Wind Gate adds a wheel that changes source-to-exit routing. Testnet only; public RPC, no indexer, no paid infrastructure. Hundreds of players and tens concurrently are a design target, not measured capacity.

## Capabilities and Constraints
First Light starts with a local preview before sign-in. Canonical actions use the recovered player's browser-authorized key, the compatible testnet registry and current ownership. Mac Privy signing and Mochi adoption are independently verified; Android same-wallet signing is user-reported. Same Mochi, earned items and progress on two physical devices remain unverified. The original operator H1 acceptance ran on testnet; it is separate from the new spatial-content acceptance. Retained signing modules include historical player-signed actions and the optional NEAR Auth recovery path; their live recovery is unverified and the earlier pet interface is retired. Receipts describe only confirmed actions. A stopped service pauses account actions without replacing the player or claiming completion.

## Brand Commitments
Familiar. A small harbor town at golden hour, picture-book warmth. Warm, plain, slightly wry language. No “wallet”, “gas”, or “tx” before a receipt; real terms are glossed when introduced. Preserve the separately requested landing page; from the title screen on, the game is an immersive world with theme-matched controls. No token prices, countdowns, buy-ins, pots or jargon in buttons. Sound is optional and off by default.

## Evidence on Hand
The user supplied `../HANDOFF.md`. `../PROGRESS.md` records actual commands and discrepancies. No deployed game, live receipts, screenshots, or successful auth integration existed at project start. Never fabricate them.

## Product Principles
- Play first; explain the chain afterwards, one sentence at a time.
- The pet leads; controls stay quiet and readable.
- Success appears only after confirmation; pending work stays pending.
- No pet dies; mood and energy recover predictably.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Text contrast at least 4.5:1, 44px minimum targets, visible focus, keyboard equivalents for every canvas action, live action feedback, and reduced-motion support. The canvas never owns essential text or controls.
